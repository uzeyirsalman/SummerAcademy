# Lesson 5: Document Design & Presentations
## The Art of Clear Communication: Briefs vs. Slide Decks

*   **Estimated Time**: 60–90 minutes
*   **Ages**: 12 & 15
*   **Key Vocabulary**: Visual Hierarchy, Serif vs. Sans-Serif, Negative (White) Space, Slide Deck, Written Brief, Cognitive Load.
*   **Prerequisites**: Google Slides, PowerPoint, Keynote, or Canva.

---

## 1. Parent Prep Guide (3-Minute Refresh)
*   **Visual Hierarchy**: The arrangement of design elements in a way that implies importance. The eye should naturally jump to the title (largest/boldest), then headings (medium), then body text (smallest).
*   **Typography**:
    *   **Serif Fonts** (e.g., Times New Roman, Georgia): Have tiny decorative feet at the ends of letters. Great for printed books and long-form reading.
    *   **Sans-Serif Fonts** (e.g., Arial, Helvetica, Inter): Clean and without feet. Best for digital screens and slide headings.
*   **Rule of Two**: Never use more than two font families in a single document/presentation (one for headings, one for body).
*   **Negative (White) Space**: The empty area around elements. It prevents "cognitive overload" (overwhelming the brain). Empty space is not wasted space; it is design breathing room.
*   **Brief vs. Slide Deck**:
    *   **Written Brief**: Meant to be read *without* a presenter. It should be highly detailed, full-sentence, structured text.
    *   **Slide Deck**: Meant to support a *live speaker*. Slides should be highly visual, using high-impact images, diagrams, and massive numbers. The biggest mistake is pasting paragraphs of text on slides and reading them aloud.

## 2. Teaching Script & Discussion Flow

### The Billboard Test
*Introduce the concept of visual communication using driving on a highway:*

*   **You (the Parent)**: "Imagine you are driving down a highway at 60 mph. You see a billboard. If the billboard has 3 paragraphs of small text describing why a local pizza shop is great, will you read it?"
*   **Teens**: "No, you'll drive right past it, or crash trying to read it!"
*   **You**: "Exactly. A billboard must convey its message in under 3 seconds. A large image of a cheesy slice of pizza and the words 'BEST PIZZA, NEXT EXIT' is all it takes."
*   **You**: "A slide deck is like a billboard. The audience should look at it, get the point in 3 seconds, and then focus their eyes back on *you* (the speaker). If they are busy reading your slide, they have stopped listening to you."
*   **You**: "On the other hand, if you are reading a newspaper, you want details. That's a **brief**. We need to know when to write a brief and when to build a deck."

### Socratic Discussion Questions
1.  *“If you are giving a school presentation and your slide has 10 lines of text, what is the audience doing while you are talking?”*
    *   **Answer**: They are reading the slide ahead of you and ignoring whatever you are saying. It splits their attention and increases their cognitive load.
2.  *“Why do you think textbooks use headings, bold text, bullet points, and images instead of just continuous solid blocks of text?”*
    *   **Answer**: To create visual hierarchy. It allows the reader's brain to scan the page, categorize information, and understand what is most important before reading every word.

## 3. Hands-On Lab: The Redesign Challenge

The kids will take a boring, wall-of-text document and present it in two distinct ways: a clean **Written Brief** and a high-impact **Slide Deck**.

### The Raw Material (The Boring Text)
Below is a raw, unformatted brief about **"The Voyager 1 Space Mission"**. Copy this block of text (or have the kids copy it):

> Voyager 1 is a space probe launched by NASA on September 5, 1977. It was part of the Voyager program to study the outer Solar System. It has been operating for decades. Its launch weight was 773 kilograms. It reached Jupiter in 1979 and Saturn in 1980. Voyager 1 is the farthest human-made object from Earth. In 2012, it became the first spacecraft to cross the heliopause and enter interstellar space. It carries a Golden Record. This record contains sounds and images selected to portray the diversity of life and culture on Earth. It is intended for any intelligent extraterrestrial life form that may find it. The probe is powered by three radioisotope thermoelectric generators (RTGs). These run on plutonium-238. They have lasted much longer than expected, but they are slowly losing power. By 2025, NASA expects to turn off the last science instruments. Voyager 1 will continue to drift through space silently for billions of years.

---

### Task 1: Format the Written Brief (Document Design)
Open a word processor (Google Docs or MS Word). Format the text above to make it professional and readable on a single page:
1.  Add a clear, large Title.
2.  Group the information into 3 logical sections with Subheadings (e.g., *Mission Overview*, *The Golden Record*, *Power and the Future*).
3.  Use bullet points for stats (weight, launch date, flybys).
4.  Apply **bolding** to 3-5 critical terms you want the reader's eye to catch first.
5.  Ensure there is healthy margin spacing (white space).

---

### Task 2: Create the 5-Slide Presentation (Slide Design)
Now, convert that same information into a 5-slide presentation deck. 

**Strict Design Rules:**
*   **Rule 1**: Maximum of **15 words** per slide!
*   **Rule 2**: Each slide must focus on *one* big idea (e.g., Slide 1: Title, Slide 2: The Distance, Slide 3: The Golden Record, Slide 4: Power Source, Slide 5: The Silent Future).
*   **Rule 3**: Use visual elements (massive numbers, e.g., "1977" or "15 Billion Miles", high-quality space photos from the web, or simple layouts).
*   **Rule 4**: Use only **two** fonts (e.g., Arial Black for titles, Calibri for text).

Have the kids present their 5-slide deck to you, explaining the story of Voyager 1.

## 4. Teens' Reflection & Log
*Have the kids answer these questions:*

1.  **Which task was harder: writing the brief or designing the slides with the 15-word limit? Why?**
2.  **How did you represent the concept of "Voyager 1 being far away" on a slide using minimal words?**
3.  **What two fonts did you choose for your slide deck, and are they Serif or Sans-Serif?**
4.  **When you presented your slides, did you look at the slides or look at your audience? How did having fewer words on screen change how you spoke?**

## 5. End-of-Lesson Quiz

1.  **What is the main purpose of "Visual Hierarchy"?**
    *   *Answer*: To guide the reader's eye to the most important information first, organizing the content logically.
2.  **When should you use a Serif font versus a Sans-Serif font?**
    *   *Answer*: Serif is best for long printed texts (like books/novels); Sans-serif is best for digital screens, titles, and slide presentations.
3.  **What is a common slide deck design mistake that increases "cognitive load"?**
    *   *Answer*: Putting paragraphs of small text on the slide, using too many different fonts, or crowding the slide with irrelevant icons/images.
4.  **True or False: If a slide deck is good, the audience should be able to understand the whole presentation without the speaker talking.**
    *   *Answer*: False. That is the definition of a written brief. A slide deck should support the speaker; it is incomplete without the speaker's words.
