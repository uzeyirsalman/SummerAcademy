Lesson 1

The Beautiful Game

What is Football & System Basics

Key Objectives

  • Understand why football is the world's most popular sport.
  • Learn the layout and key markings of a standard pitch.
  • Identify the four core player positions and match structures.

Press to enter the lesson.

Lesson 1: Introduction

Introduce the subject. Explain that football requires almost no gear (just a ball and feet) which is why it spread globally.

The Pitch Geometry

Core boundaries of the rectangle field

Key Areas

• **Centre Circle:** Match kick-offs and post-goal restarts occur here.
• **Penalty Box:** Defending fouls here result in penalty kicks. Goalkeepers can use hands here.
• **Six-Yard Box:** Goal kicks are taken from here.

Dimensions

• Standard size is **105m long by 68m wide**.
• Goal is **7.32m wide** and **2.44m high**.
• Crossbar is the top horizontal beam; posts are the side vertical beams.

Teacher prompt

Socratic: "Why is the penalty box so critical? What does it give the goalkeeper?" (Hand privileges, and penalty kick consequence for defensive fouls).

The Four Core Positions

Goalkeeper (GK)

Only player allowed to use hands, inside their box. Wears different jersey colors to stand out from referees and players.

Defenders (DF)

Stops opposing attacks. Centre-backs block the middle; full-backs defend the sides and start counters.

Midfielders (MF)

The engine room. Defensive "six" blocks threats; central "eight" connects lines; creative "ten" makes plays.

Forwards (FW)

Striker "nine" leads the charge; wingers supply crosses from out wide. Kylian Mbappé attacks from the wide-left forward spot.

Ensure kids understand numbers: 6 is defensive mid, 8 is box-to-box, 10 is playmaker, 9 is main striker.

How Match Time Works

Time Blocks

• **90 minutes** total, split into two 45-minute halves.
• **Injury Time (Stoppage Time):** Added by the referee to make up for injuries/subs (2-10 min).
• **Knock-out Extra Time:** 2 periods of 15 minutes if tied in knockout rounds.

Scoring

• Goal scored when the **entire ball** crosses the **entire goal line**.
• Checked instantly by goal-line sensors.
• Tied knockout games after extra time are decided by a penalty shootout.

Tell the story of how a ball can bounce back out but still count if it fully crossed the line.

Lesson 1 Review

Socratic Scenarios & Lab

  1. A goalkeeper runs out of his penalty box to block a shot, and punches the ball away. What is the referee's call?
  2. A ball hit the crossbar, bounced straight down onto the white line, and spun back into the goalie's hands. Does it count as a goal?
  3. **Lab:** Sketch a pitch map with labels for the centre circle, penalty box, six-yard box, posts, and crossbar.

Answers: 1) Handballs outside the box are illegal for GK, resulting in a direct free kick (and likely a card). 2) No goal—it must cross the line completely.

Lesson 2

Rules You Must Know

Understanding Offsides, Fouls, Cards, and VAR

Key Objectives

  • Deconstruct the offside rule and VAR review procedures.
  • Differentiate between direct and indirect free kick fouls.
  • Understand the penalty shootout mechanics and bookings.

This is the most important rules lesson. Focus heavily on Offside as it is the most active debate in matches.

The Offside Rule Demystified

Second-to-last defender boundary

1. Offside Position

A player is in an offside position if they are in the **opponent's half** AND closer to the goal line than **both the ball and the second-to-last defender**.

2. Active Play Offense

Being offside is only penalized if the player **actively participates** at the exact moment the ball is kicked forward by a teammate.

**VAR Freeze Line:** Checked to the millimeter. If any scoring body part (shoulder, knee, toe) is ahead of the defender at the kick moment, they are offside.

Example: Mbappé stands behind the last defender. Midfielder kicks a pass. When kicked, Mbappé was past the line. Offside called.

Fouls and Restarts

Common Fouls

• Tripping or pushing opponents.
• Sliding tackles hitting the player before the ball.
• Deliberately touching the ball with hand/arm (Handball).

Restarts

• **Direct Free Kick:** Can be shot directly into the net.
• **Indirect Free Kick:** Must touch another player before crossing the goal line.
• **Penalty Kick:** Awarded if a defender commits a foul inside their penalty box.

Explain that free kicks let the attacking team take control back. A wall of defenders must stand 9.15m away.

Corner, Throw, and Goal Kicks

Corner Kick

If the defending team kicks the ball out over their own goal line, the attackers restart from the corner arc.

Throw-In

If the ball goes out the long sides, play restarts by throwing with both hands from behind the head, feet on the ground.

Goal Kick

If attackers send the ball out over the opponent's goal line, the defenders restart from the six-yard box.

Crucial to emphasize that throw-in rules are strict: lifting a foot is a foul throw and switches possession.

Bookings & VAR Reviews

Bookings

• **Yellow Card:** Warning for serious fouls or time-wasting. Two yellows in a match = Red card send-off.
• **Red Card:** Immediate ejection. Team plays short a player (10 men). Player banned next match.

VAR Scope

Reviews only four critical scenarios:
1. Goals and build-up offenses.
2. Penalty awards.
3. Direct Red Card incidents.
4. Mistaken player identity.

Tell the story of how VAR reviews slow down the game but fix clear and obvious errors.

Penalty Shootout Rules

Settling tied knockout matches

Penalty Kick

Shot from the penalty spot (11m). Only GK and kicker allowed inside the area. GK must keep feet on the goal line until kicked. Statistically scored ~75% of the time.

Shootout Structure

• 5 players from each team alternate shots.
• If tied after 5 rounds, shootout enters sudden death.
• France has historic penalty woes (lost 2022 World Cup Final to Argentina on penalties).

Ask: "How did Argentina beat France in 2022?" (Mbappé scored a hat-trick in the game, but Argentina won the shootout after France missed kicks).

Lesson 2 Review

Socratic Scenarios

  1. A player passes the ball forward to his striker. The striker was onside when the ball was passed, but ran past the last defender before catching the ball. Is he offside?
  2. A defender slides in to tackle Mbappé inside the box, completely missing the ball and tripping Mbappé. What is the referee's decision?
  3. True or False: If a player gets a yellow card in the first half and another in the second half, they can stay on the field.
  4. **Lab:** Analyze a freeze frame (imaginary scenario) where a player's shoulder is past the defender but their feet are onside. Are they offside?

Answers: 1) Onside—offside is judged at the moment of the pass. 2) Penalty kick for France. 3) False—2 yellows = red card ejection. 4) Offside—shoulders can legally score.

Lesson 3

The World Cup

History, Qualifiers, and the 2026 Expansion

Key Objectives

  • Detail the history of the FIFA World Cup since 1930.
  • Explain continental qualification systems.
  • Understand the expanded 48-team format for 2026.

Discuss the scale of the World Cup: 1.5 billion people watched the 2022 final.

Historic Milestones

Year Host Significant Match / Milestone
1930 Uruguay First World Cup. Uruguay hosted and won against Argentina. Only 13 teams.
1950 Brazil "Maracanazo". Uruguay stunned hosts Brazil 2-1 in front of 200,000 in Rio.
1970 Mexico Pelé's final tournament. Brazil played beautiful football, winning their 3rd title.
2014 Brazil Germany destroyed hosts Brazil 7-1 in the semi-finals, shocking the world.
2022 Qatar Messi's Argentina beat France on penalties in the greatest final ever.

Use the Brazil 7-1 game or the Maracanazo upset to show that football has dramatic, historical impacts on host nations.

Continental Qualifications

211 member countries competing to enter the tournament

How Zones Work

Every region runs groups or leagues over 2 years:
• **Europe (UEFA):** Groups, top teams qualify.
• **South America (CONMEBOL):** Single league, top 6 qualify directly.
• **Italy Shock:** Italy failed to qualify for both 2018 and 2022!

2026 Expansion

• **48 teams** compete (up from 32).
• **Group Stage:** 12 groups of 4. Top 2 plus 8 best 3rd-place teams advance.
• **Knockouts:** Starts at Round of 32 (adds one extra knockout round).

Ask: "Why did FIFA expand to 48 teams?" (Allows more developing nations to play, increases global interest and revenue).

2026 Co-Hosts & Records

Triple Hosts

• Co-hosted by **United States, Canada, and Mexico**.
• First three-nation host setup.
• Final will be at **MetLife Stadium** in New Jersey (82,500 seats).
• Mexico is the first 3-time host (1970, 1986, 2026).

Key Records

• **Most Titles:** Brazil with 5 wins.
• **Most Single-Cup Goals:** Just Fontaine (France)—13 goals in 1958.
• **Most Overall Goals:** Miroslav Klose (Germany)—16 goals.
• **Fastest Goal:** 11 seconds (Hakan Şükür, Turkey, 2002).

Tell the students about Just Fontaine's 13-goal record, which has survived for 70 years because modern defenses are much tighter.

Lesson 3 Review

Socratic Scenarios

  1. Which nation has won the most World Cup titles?
  2. What is the expansion format for the 2026 World Cup? How many teams are entering?
  3. Which three countries are hosting the 2026 World Cup? Where is the final being held?
  4. **Lab:** Research the 16 host cities of the 2026 World Cup. Identify which cities are hosting matches in Canada and Mexico.

Answers: 1) Brazil (5). 2) 48 teams, 12 groups of 4, Round of 32 knockouts. 3) USA, Canada, Mexico. MetLife Stadium, NJ. 4) Canada: Vancouver, Toronto. Mexico: CDMX, Monterrey, Guadalajara.

Les Bleus — Your Team

History, Zidane's Era, and Deschamps' Tactics

Key Objectives

  • Trace the milestones of the France National Team (Les Bleus).
  • Understand the cultural impact of the 1998 "Black, Blanc, Beur" win.
  • Analyze France's current 4-3-3 counter-attacking play style.

Explain that since the user is supporting France, this lesson gives them the complete history of their team.

The French Legacy

1998 Champions

• France hosted and won their first World Cup, beating Brazil 3-0 in the final.
• **Zidane** scored two headers; **Thierry Henry** starred.
• Celebrated as **"Black, Blanc, Beur"** (Black, White, Arab)—a symbol of multicultural unity.

Peaks & Valleys

• **Euro 2000:** Won back-to-back.
• **2002:** Humiliated—out in group stage without scoring.
• **2006 Final:** Zidane's Panenka penalty, followed by the infamous headbutt red card on Materazzi. Lost to Italy.

Zidane's headbutt is one of the most famous red cards in history. Discuss the drama of his last professional match.

2018 Victory & Current Style

2018 & 2022 Qatar

• **2018 Champion:** Beating Croatia 4-2 in Russia. 19yo Mbappé scored in the final.
• **2022 Final:** Mbappé scored a historic hat-trick to equalize 3-3, but lost to Argentina on penalties.

Tactical Profile

• Managed by **Didier Deschamps** (1998 captain).
• Play style: Defensive solidity combined with blistering **counter-attacks**.
• Target: Absorb pressure, win the ball, and release Mbappé into open space.

Socratic: "Why does France sit deep instead of attacking all the time?" (Saves energy, and draws opponents forward, leaving massive space behind them for Mbappé to sprint into).

  3. Explain what happened in the 2006 World Cup Final involving Zinedine Zidane.
  4. **Lab:** Compare France's 1998 starting lineup with their 2018 starting lineup. Which squad had more creative depth in midfield?

Answers: 1) Les Bleus, due to blue jerseys. Stade de France. 2) "Black, Blanc, Beur". 3) Zidane scored a Panenka, then headbutted Materazzi and got a red card. Italy won on penalties. 4) 1998 had Zidane/Deschamps; 2018 had Pogba/Kanté. Both highly balanced.

Lesson 7

Reading a Match

Formations, Tactical Concepts, and expected Goals (xG)

Key Objectives

  • Identify standard tactical shapes (4-3-3, 4-4-2, 3-5-2).
  • Understand pressing traps, high defensive lines, and deep blocks.
  • Explain expected Goals (xG) and PPDA statistics.

Teach the kids how to analyze the TV screen tactically instead of just chasing the ball with their eyes.

Common Match Shapes

Back 4 Formations

• **4-3-3:** France shape. Width via wide forwards. High attacking numbers.
• **4-4-2:** Classic English shape. Two banks of four, two strikers. Solid but congested in midfield.
• **4-2-3-1:** Standard modern shape. Two deep midfielders protect the back, three creators support one striker.

Back 3 Formations

• **3-5-2:** Three central defenders, two wing-backs covering the whole flank, three midfielders, two strikers.
• **Transition:** Formations are flexible. A 4-3-3 in attack often shifts to a 4-5-1 when defending.

Explain that wing-backs are hybrid defender-midfielders who must run the entire length of the field.

Core Tactical Terms

Defending Blocks

• **High Press:** Attackers aggressively tackle defenders near the opponent's goal to force errors.
• **Parking the Bus:** Deep block defending, packing 10 men in the box. No spaces allowed.

Lines & Counters

• **High Line:** Defenders push to the halfway circle, catching strikers offside. Risky if space is exploited.
• **Counter-Attack:** Defending deep then launching fast long balls into space for forwards.

Socratic: "What happens if a high press team gets tired?" (Defenders get isolated, and opponents pass right through them).

Decoded: expected Goals (xG)

Measuring the mathematical probability of a shot

What is xG?

• Rating from **0.00 to 1.00** representing the chance of scoring.
• Calculated from historical shot data (distance, angle, defenders, foot/head).
• **0.70 xG:** High chance (e.g. penalty kick). Expect to score 7 out of 10 times.
• **0.03 xG:** Speculative 30m shot. Expect to score 3 out of 100 times.

Other Stats

• **xG Underperformance:** Creating good chances but failing to finish them.
• **PPDA (Passes Allowed):** Measures press intensity. Lower number = more aggressive press.
• **Parking the bus:** Deep defensive shield.

Explain that xG helps commentators analyze matches objectively. A team might lose 1-0 but have an xG of 2.5 vs the opponent's 0.2.

Lesson 7 Review

Socratic Scenarios

  1. Explain what a 4-3-3 formation looks like. How many midfielders are on the pitch?
  2. A team has a low possession percentage (30%) but wins the game 3-0. What tactics did they likely use?
  3. Striker shoots from the halfway line. What would the expected Goals (xG) rating of this shot look like, and why?
  4. **Lab:** Watch a match highlight clip. Identify the defensive shape of both teams and calculate their approximate formation.

Answers: 1) 4 defenders, 3 midfielders, 3 forwards. 3 midfielders. 2) Counter-attacking deep block ("parking the bus"). 3) Extremely low (e.g., 0.01 xG) because of the distance. 4) Class exercises.

Clubs and Leagues

Domestic Leagues, Nicknames, and Relegation Stakes

Key Objectives

  • Differentiate between club matches and national team schedules.
  • Compare the "Big Five" European leagues and their profiles.
  • Understand the drama of promotion and relegation.

This slide covers club football, which runs 10 months a year and holds 95% of a player's active time.

The Big Five Leagues

Premier League (England)

The richest league in the world. High intensity, fast pace. Clubs: Man City, Arsenal, Liverpool, Man Utd.

La Liga (Spain)

Technically refined, passing game. Dominant clubs: Real Madrid and Barcelona.

Bundesliga (Germany)

Highest fan attendance. High-pressing game. Dominant club: Bayern Munich.

Serie A (Italy) & Ligue 1 (France)

Italy: defensive tactics, Juventus/Milan. France: PSG dominant force; intense derbies.

Mention that players migrate between these leagues through transfers (covered next lesson).

Famous Clubs & Nicknames

Club Country Nickname Origin / Kit color
Real Madrid Spain Los Blancos White jersey.
FC Barcelona Spain Blaugrana / Barça Blue and red stripes.
Manchester United England The Red Devils Red jersey, devil on badge.
Arsenal England The Gunners Founded by munitions factory workers.
Juventus Italy The Old Lady Long tradition and history.
Paris Saint-Germain France PSG / Les Parisiens Paris capital club.

Point out the Arsenal Gunners origin—shows how deeply tied clubs are to local histories.

Relegation Drama & Champions League

Promotion / Relegation

• Bottom 3 teams are **relegated** to the lower division next season.
• Top teams from lower division are **promoted**.
• Makes every match crucial. Non-existent in American franchise leagues (NFL/NBA).

Champions League (UCL)

• Top clubs from all European leagues compete annually.
• **The Double:** Winning league + UCL.
• **The Treble:** Winning league + UCL + domestic cup (extremely rare).

Discuss the pressure of relegation—losing a spot in the Premier League costs a club over £100 million in TV rights.

Lesson 9

Transfers and the Money

Deals, Agent Commissions, Free Transfers, and FFP

Key Objectives

  • Explain how a club-to-club transfer works step-by-step.
  • Contrast transfer fees, loans, and free transfers (Bosman).
  • Understand revenue sources and Financial Fair Play (FFP) boundaries.

Explain that transfers are how squads are built. High-finance deals are now key parts of the sport's entertainment.

How a Transfer Works

1. Bid & Terms

• **Bidding:** Club A submits a fee offer to Club B.
• **Windows:** Deals can only register during Summer (June-Aug) and Winter (Jan) windows.
• **Personal Terms:** Player and agent negotiate weekly wages and contract length.

2. Closing the Deal

• **Medical:** Player undergoes physical checkups. Injury risks can collapse deals.
• **Signing:** Contract signed. Announcement videos are released.
• **Agent Commission:** Agents take ~10% cut of transfer fees or player contract value.

Discuss the medical check—explain how knee cartilage issues can halt a €50 million transfer.

Loans, Free Transfers & Records

Alternative Deals

• **Loan Deal:** Temporary move (6-12 months). Club B pays wages. Great for young players.
• **Free Transfer:** Contract expired. No transfer fee is paid. Player gets a high signing bonus (e.g. Mbappé to Real Madrid, 2024).

Record Fees

• **Highest Ever:** Neymar (€222m, Barca to PSG, 2017). Changed the market.
• **Mbappé to PSG:** €180m (2018).
• **FFP Rules:** Clubs cannot spend significantly more than they earn.

Free transfers are named "Bosman transfers" after Jean-Marc Bosman, who fought for players' rights in court in 1995.

Lesson 9 Review

Socratic Scenarios

  1. Explain the difference between a transfer fee and a player's wage. Who receives the transfer fee?
  2. What is a "free transfer," and how does it benefit the signing player financially?
  3. Why did Neymar's €222 million transfer in 2017 change the global transfer market?
  4. **Lab:** You are a club director. You have a €50m budget. Do you buy one superstar striker for €50m, or three solid squad players for €15m each? Balance your squad.

Answers: 1) Transfer fee goes club-to-club; wage goes to the player. 2) No fee is paid; player negotiates higher wages/signing bonuses. 3) Doubled the previous record, causing inflation. 4) Class discussion.

The Legendary Immortals
